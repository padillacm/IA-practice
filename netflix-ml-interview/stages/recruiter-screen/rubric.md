# Rubric: recruiter-screen

L5 is the anchor level; L4 and L6 bars are extrapolated from it. Netflix publishes no common scale.

## Shared definitions
- **Yes**: the observable behavior in the level's row of the competency table occurs in the candidate's own words.
- **No**: the candidate partly reaches the bar (some elements present, one or more missing) or reaches it only after
  a nudge.
- **Strong No**: the candidate contradicts the bar (for example cites salary as the only reason, or says they have
  not read the memo and shows no curiosity about it) or needed a substantive hint.
- **Strong Yes**: the candidate meets the Yes bar of the next level up, with specific evidence.
- No evidence: the topic never came up for a candidate-side reason outside their control.

## motivation_why_netflix
core: true

Looks for a reason tied to a concrete technical or product problem, not to brand or pay.

| Level | Yes bar |
|---|---|
| L4 | Names one Netflix product area or published work (for example recommendations, experimentation, encoding) and links it to a project they did or want to learn; does not lead with salary or prestige. |
| L5 | Names a specific problem space at Netflix, explains in two or more sentences why it fits their experience, and states what they would want to change or learn; mentions a culture trait with an example from their own work. |
| L6 | Describes an organization-level problem they want to take on, connects it to scope they have led, and contrasts Netflix's approach with at least one alternative they considered; motivation is stated independent of compensation. |

## background_clarity
core: true

| Level | Yes bar |
|---|---|
| L4 | Walks through their last one or two roles in under three minutes, naming what they built, the tools used and one outcome. |
| L5 | Tells a chronological arc with owned scope, one quantified outcome and a clear answer to "why now" that points forward (what they want next) without blaming past employers. |
| L6 | Tells an arc showing direction-setting across teams, quantifies outcomes at team or org level, and explains why the next move expands that scope. |

## memo_awareness
core: true

| Level | Yes bar |
|---|---|
| L4 | Says they read the memo, names at least two of its values or principles correctly, and gives one reaction in their own words. |
| L5 | Names a memo idea (for example context not control, keeper test, farming for dissent, informed captain) and gives an example from their own work where they behaved that way or did not; states one aspect they are unsure about. |
| L6 | Compares a memo principle to how they have run decisions or teams, names a trade-off the principle creates (for example loose coupling versus alignment) and says how they would handle it. |

## role_fit_logistics
core: false

| Level | Yes bar |
|---|---|
| L4 | States location, work authorization and earliest start date, and asks one question about the team or level. |
| L5 | States logistics and other active processes, gives a compensation expectation tied to the role and location without anchoring on a number alone, and confirms the level they are targeting with a reason. |
| L6 | States logistics and other processes, discusses level and scope expectations with reference to prior impact, and asks how team matching and the loop vary for senior roles. |

## culture_signals
core: false
memo_values: [candor, curiosity, selflessness]

| Level | Yes bar |
|---|---|
| L4 | Mentions asking for feedback or learning from a mistake in an example without blaming others. |
| L5 | Gives a concrete example of receiving or giving direct feedback and credits others by name for a shared result. |
| L6 | Gives an example of admitting an error publicly to a team and states what changed in how they work afterwards. |
