# Question bank: recruiter-screen

Topics reported for this round: background, why a change, "Why Netflix?", memo reading, logistics, compensation
(research.md 2 Recruiter screen). These are original prompts built from those topics, not recalled questions.

### recruiter-screen-why-01
```yaml
roles: [mle, rs, ds]
levels: [L4, L5, L6]
variant: default
domain: any
kind: main
signal_points:
  - reason tied to a specific technical or product problem
  - does not lead with salary, brand or perks
  - connects the reason to own experience
```
Why Netflix, and why this role specifically? Pick the one problem you would most want to work on.

Follow-ups:
- Which Netflix product surface or published work did you look at before this call?
- What would make you decline an offer from here even if the pay were top of market?
- What would you want to learn here that you cannot learn where you are?

### recruiter-screen-why-02
```yaml
roles: [mle, rs, ds]
levels: [L4, L5, L6]
variant: default
domain: recsys
kind: main
signal_points:
  - names a personalization problem (ranking, cold start, artwork, long-term satisfaction)
  - links to own past work
  - shows awareness of a published Netflix approach without reciting figures
```
You mentioned recommendations. What is it about personalization at a catalog like Netflix's that attracts you, compared
with personalization in a shopping or social product?

Follow-ups:
- Where do you think the hardest part of the problem is: data, objectives, or evaluation?
- Have you read any Netflix technical post or paper? What was your reaction?

### recruiter-screen-why-03
```yaml
roles: [ds, rs]
levels: [L4, L5, L6]
variant: default
domain: experimentation
kind: main
signal_points:
  - ties interest to experimentation or causal inference at scale
  - mentions a trade-off (sensitivity, interference, long-term outcomes)
  - own experience with experiments
```
A lot of the work in analytics and experimentation here is about deciding what to ship from noisy evidence. What draws you
to that, and what in your experience prepared you for it?

Follow-ups:
- What is the hardest experiment decision you made that went against a stakeholder's preference?
- What did you learn from an experiment that surprised you?

### recruiter-screen-background-01
```yaml
roles: [mle, rs, ds]
levels: [L4, L5, L6]
variant: default
domain: any
kind: main
signal_points:
  - clear chronological arc in under three minutes
  - owned scope and a quantified outcome
  - relevance to the role
```
Walk me through your background in about three minutes: what you work on today, what you own, and the result you are proudest of.

Follow-ups:
- What part of that was yours versus the team's?
- How did you measure that it worked?
- Which part of that would be the closest to what you would do here?

### recruiter-screen-whynow-01
```yaml
roles: [mle, rs, ds]
levels: [L4, L5, L6]
variant: default
domain: any
kind: main
signal_points:
  - forward-looking reason
  - no blame toward current employer
  - connects the move to growth in scope or depth
```
What made you start looking now, and what are you hoping the next role gives you that the current one does not?

Follow-ups:
- If your current team offered you that tomorrow, would you stay?
- How would your manager describe why you are leaving?
- Are you talking with other companies? What stage are you at?

### recruiter-screen-memo-01
```yaml
roles: [mle, rs, ds]
levels: [L4, L5, L6]
variant: default
domain: any
kind: main
signal_points:
  - has read the memo (names specific ideas correctly)
  - example from own behavior, not a paraphrase
  - one honest reservation or question
```
Netflix's culture memo is a big part of how we hire. Which part of it stood out to you, and where have you seen yourself behave that way, or not?

Follow-ups:
- The memo talks about "context not control". What does that look like for someone in your seat?
- Which statement in the memo would you find hardest to live with?
- Can you name the keeper test and say how you would feel being measured by it?

### recruiter-screen-memo-02
```yaml
roles: [mle, rs, ds]
levels: [L5, L6]
variant: default
domain: any
kind: main
signal_points:
  - understands informed captain and farming for dissent
  - gives an example of a decision made that way
  - names a risk of the approach
```
The memo says Netflix avoids decision-making by committee and relies on an informed captain who farms for dissent. Tell me about a decision you drove in that way, or one that would have gone better if you had.

Follow-ups:
- How did you find the people who would disagree?
- What happened after the decision: did you write down what you learned?

### recruiter-screen-logistics-01
```yaml
roles: [mle, rs, ds]

variant: default
domain: any
kind: main
signal_points:
  - location, authorization, start date stated
```

- Is there a deadline from another process I should know about?

### recruiter-screen-comp-01
```yaml
roles: [mle, rs, ds]
levels: [L4, L5, L6]
variant: default
domain: any
kind: main
signal_points:
  - gives expectation without leading with it as motivation
  - understands Netflix pays top of market for the role and location
  - confirms target level with reasoning
```
What are you expecting for compensation, and what level are you targeting? For context, Netflix pays "personal top of market for the role and location".

Follow-ups:
- How do you decide what top of market is for you?
- How would you weigh a lower number with more scope against the reverse?
- What do you think the level difference between L5 and L6 is in terms of scope?
