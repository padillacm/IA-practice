# 16-Week Intensive Plan: B2 → C1

**Target:** about 11 hours/week · **Focus:** accuracy, advanced grammar, vocabulary/collocations, listening, speaking · **Goal:** real-world C1 (no exam)

Cambridge English estimates about **200 guided learning hours** per CEFR level. This plan gives about 175 structured hours, and
the listening, reading and conversation you do for pleasure pushes you past 200. Measure progress with the diagnostic and
your speaking numbers, not with hours alone.

---

## The four strands (why the week looks like this)

Research on language learning (Paul Nation's *four strands*) shows that progress needs a **balance** of four kinds of work.
Too much studying *about* English, and you'll know a lot but still hesitate when you speak. Your weak areas are speaking and
listening, so **real input and output get half of your time**.

| Strand | Hours/week | What it is here |
|---|---|---|
| 🎧📖 **Meaning-focused input** | ~3 h | podcasts, series, YouTube (2 h) + reading articles on the week's topic (1 h) |
| 🗣️ **Meaning-focused output** | ~3 h | 3 conversations (tutor / exchange / AI) + daily recorded speaking with feedback |
| 📘 **Language-focused learning** | ~3.5 h | notebooks, flashcards, dictation, mixed review, mistake bank |
| ⚡ **Fluency development** | ~1.5 h | 4-3-2, shadowing, re-recording the same task, retelling what you heard |

## The weekly topic cycle

Each week has **one topic** (work & AI, cities, health, money, travel, education, climate, technology, culture...).
All four strands feed on the same topic, so you meet the same words again and again, which is how they stick.

| Day | Topic work |
|---|---|
| Mon | Listen to a podcast episode on the topic + worksheet (Notebook 06 §4) |
| Tue | Read 1-2 articles on the topic; harvest 10 chunks into `my_words` |
| Wed | 4-3-2 on a topic prompt (`four_three_two()`), recorded |
| Thu | Conversation with a tutor, exchange partner or AI **on the same topic** |
| Fri | Re-record Wednesday's task; compare `speech_stats()`. Write 150-200 words on the topic (accuracy check, see below) |
| Sat | Extensive listening/watching for pleasure + one conversation |
| Sun | Notebook 09: weekly report, mistake bank, reflection, choose next week's topic |

## A typical weekday (~90 min)

```
07:30  12'  Flashcards: study([...]) - 8 new cards/day max (Notebook 08)
08:00  30'  Podcast on the commute (extensive): no pausing, just enjoy
13:30  20'  Intensive listening: a dictation or micro-dictations (Notebook 06)
19:00  25'  Notebook of the week: one section + exercise + check()
19:25   2'  Pronunciation warm-up: one minimal-pair column + one tongue twister
19:27  15'  Speaking: shadowing (Mon/Wed/Fri) or a recorded prompt with the feedback loop (Tue/Thu)
       +1'  log_study(...)
Mon/Wed/Fri +10'  mixed_review() and review_mistakes()  (from week 3)
```

## Phase 4: Consolidation & real-world use (Weeks 13–16)
*Goal: make everything automatic under pressure, without losing what you studied in weeks 1–12.*

Every week in this phase: `mixed_review()` 3×/week · `review_mistakes()` daily · one NB01b redo · the weekly topic cycle ·
re-record your **week-1 benchmark prompt** and compare `speech_stats()`.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 13 | Mostly output: 4 conversations this week. After each one, log 3 "gaps" (things you couldn't say) in `my_words` and your errors with `log_mistake()` |
| 14 | Native content only (no learner materials): a book or long reads, a series, podcasts. Keep a chunk notebook |
| 15 | Give a 10-minute talk in English (Toastmasters, work, or recorded) and get feedback. Redo all sets < 90% |
| 16 | **Final diagnostic (Form A)** + can-do checklist + compare the week-0 and week-16 recordings. Optional external check: EF SET or a Cambridge C1 sample test. Plan the next 16 weeks toward C1+/C2 |

## Weekly writing-for-accuracy (30 min, Friday)
Writing isn't your goal, but it's the best way to catch grammar errors: you have time to notice them.
Write 150–200 words on the week's topic. Ask an AI tool (or Write & Improve) to check **only** your weak areas: articles,
prepositions, tenses, and the structures of the current notebook. Log each error with `log_mistake()`, then read the
corrected text aloud and retell it without looking.

## Rules that make the difference
1. **Record yourself** at least 3 times a week and measure it (`speech_stats()`). It's uncomfortable, and it's the fastest feedback loop you have.
2. **Chunks, not words.** Every new expression goes into a flashcard with its collocation and an example.
3. **Mistakes are data.** The mistake bank (`review_mistakes()`) and `log_mistake()` make your personal syllabus.
4. **Output every day.** Say every example aloud, and use each new structure in a sentence about *your* life.
5. **Recycle.** `mixed_review()` 3×/week keeps weeks 1–4 alive in week 14.
6. **Enjoy the input.** Pick podcasts and series you'd watch anyway. Hours of input you actually enjoy are the fuel.
7. **Missed a day?** Just do flashcards + 10 min of listening. Never miss two days in a row.

## If you have less time some weeks
Minimum viable day (30 min): flashcards 10' + dictation or podcast 10' + one recorded speaking prompt 10'.
