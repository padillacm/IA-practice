# Resources: B2 → C1

A curated list, filtered for your priorities (**grammar, vocabulary/collocations, listening, speaking**) and for real-world use.
⭐ = highest recommendation.

## 📚 Books

### Grammar
- ⭐ **Advanced Grammar in Use** (Martin Hewings, Cambridge UP). The C1 grammar reference and workbook; pairs directly with Notebooks 01–02.
- ⭐ **Practical English Usage** (Michael Swan, Oxford UP). The best reference for "why is this wrong?" questions.
- **Destination C1 & C2: Grammar and Vocabulary** (Mann & Taylore-Knowles, Macmillan). Lots of exercises, including collocations and word formation.
- **Oxford Practice Grammar – Advanced** (George Yule, Oxford UP).

### Vocabulary
- ⭐ **English Collocations in Use – Advanced** (O'Dell & McCarthy, Cambridge UP)
- **English Vocabulary in Use – Advanced** (McCarthy & O'Dell)
- **English Phrasal Verbs in Use – Advanced** and **English Idioms in Use – Advanced** (Cambridge UP)
- **Oxford Word Skills – Advanced** (Gairns & Redman)
- **Business Vocabulary in Use – Advanced** (Bill Mascull, Cambridge UP). Useful if you use English at work.

### Pronunciation
- **English Pronunciation in Use – Advanced** (Martin Hewings, Cambridge UP)
- **Sound Foundations** (Adrian Underhill). Useful for understanding how English sounds work.
- **A Syllabus for Listening: Decoding** (Richard Cauldwell). The key reference on understanding fast speech; see also his *Cool Speech* materials (speechinaction.org).

## 🔎 Dictionaries & corpus tools (use them every day)
- ⭐ **Oxford Collocations Dictionary**. Look up any noun or verb to see its natural partners. An unofficial free copy of the 1st edition is widely used at **ozdic.com**; the current edition is in print and in the app.
- ⭐ **Cambridge Dictionary** (dictionary.cambridge.org). UK/US audio, IPA, CEFR level of each meaning, and a thesaurus ("SMART Vocabulary").
- **Oxford Learner's Dictionaries** (oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com) and **Longman** (ldoceonline.com). Good for collocations and example sentences.
- **SkELL** (skell.sketchengine.eu). Shows real example sentences and "word sketches" of collocations from a large corpus.
- **English Vocabulary Profile** (englishprofile.org, free registration). Tells you the CEFR level of words and phrases, so you can find C1 vocabulary.
- **Netspeak** (netspeak.org) / **Ludwig** (ludwig.guru, limited free searches). Check which version of a phrase people actually use.

| **BBC Radio 4** (*More or Less*, *In Our Time*) via BBC Sounds | British, intellectual, fast | C1–C2 |

**Series and films** (start with English subtitles, then remove them): *Ted Lasso*, *The Office* (US/UK), *Friends*,
*Modern Family*, *Brooklyn Nine-Nine*, *The Crown*, *Sherlock*, *Fleabag*, *Derry Girls* (Northern Irish accent challenge!).
*Friends* is still useful, but some of its 1990s slang is dated.
For **workplace English**: *W1A* (BBC satire of British corporate jargon), *Industry* (London banking), *Severance* (American corporate satire),
*Silicon Valley* (tech jargon), *Succession* (boardroom English; lots of swearing).
Panel shows for fast, spontaneous speech: *Would I Lie to You?*, *Taskmaster*, *QI*.

### Listening tools
- ⭐ **Language Reactor** (browser extension for Netflix/YouTube). Dual subtitles, sentence-by-sentence replay, and saves vocabulary.
- ⭐ **YouGlish** (youglish.com). Type any word or phrase and hear it in hundreds of real YouTube clips, filterable by accent.
- **Podcast apps with transcripts** (Apple Podcasts and Spotify show auto-transcripts for many shows).
- **IDEA, the International Dialects of English Archive** (dialectsarchive.com). Recordings of accents from all over the world.
- **edge-tts** (free neural text-to-speech with real accents). The notebooks use it automatically when it's installed.

## 🗣️ Speaking & pronunciation
- ⭐ **italki**, **Preply**. One-to-one tutors. Ask for "C1 conversation practice with written corrections after the lesson".
- **Cambly**. Mostly unstructured chat with native speakers: good for fluency, less correction.
- **Tandem**, **HelloTalk**. Free language exchange apps.
- **Meetup.com** language exchanges; **Toastmasters International** (use the club finder to find an English-language club near you), the best place for C1+ public speaking practice.
- **Rachel's English** (YouTube, American pronunciation), **English with Lucy** and **Pronunciation Studio** (British).
- **BBC Learning English – *Tim's Pronunciation Workshop***. Short videos on weak forms, linking and elision.
- **Mark Hancock – PronPack** (hancockmcdonald.com). Free pronunciation games and worksheets.
- **Sounds: The Pronunciation App** (Macmillan). Interactive IPA chart with UK/US audio.
- **Praat** or any pitch-tracking app, to *see* your intonation next to a model.
- **faster-whisper** (local transcription). Used by `analyse_recording()` in Notebook 07 to transcribe your recordings.
- **ELSA Speak** (app). Automatic pronunciation feedback.
- **Forvo** (forvo.com). Pronunciations recorded by native speakers.
- **AI conversation practice**: use a voice-mode assistant with the prompt in Notebook 07 §5. It works well for daily, low-pressure practice and feedback.

## 📖 Reading (supports vocabulary, and it's where collocations live)
- **The Guardian Long Read**, **The Atlantic**, **Aeon** (aeon.co), **The Economist**, **BBC Future**, **Longreads**.
- Novels with natural contemporary English: Kazuo Ishiguro, Sally Rooney, Nick Hornby, Mark Haddon, Andy Weir.
- Method: read for pleasure, highlight **chunks** (not single words), and add 5 per article to your deck.

## ✍️ Writing (not a focus right now, but useful)
- **Write & Improve** (writeandimprove.com, Cambridge). Free automatic feedback on your writing with a CEFR estimate.
- **Grammarly** / **LanguageTool**. Catch errors, but always understand *why* before accepting a change.

## 📏 Measuring progress
- **CEFR Companion Volume** (Council of Europe, 2020). The official "can-do" descriptors; the checklist in Notebook 09 is based on them.
- **EF SET** (efset.org). A free reading and listening test (about 50 minutes; there is also a shorter quick check) with a CEFR result.
- **Vocabulary Size Test** (Nation & Beglar). Free online versions estimate how many word families you know - a useful benchmark for vocabulary breadth.
- **Cambridge C1 Advanced sample papers** (cambridgeenglish.org). Useful as a benchmark even if you don't take the exam.
